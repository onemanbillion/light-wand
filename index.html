4 · Size and motion

Hold the phone at arm's length, lock your wrist and turn your whole body. This is the most accurate mode.

Slide the phone in a straight line, along a table edge or rail if you can, and pause briefly at each end.

No sensors. Move at a steady pace; a beep marks each end of the image.

Left and right are as seen by the camera. Light strip 0 lights the whole screen as a moving window: brighter, but it needs good tracking. Most recent iPhones are 460 PPI; mini models are 476.

5 · Shoot

The countdown shows big numbers for the photographer. When the screen goes black and the phone beeps, open the shutter. After the pause, painting starts with a short beep; a double beep means it has finished. Double-tap the screen to stop early.

How to get a good shot
  • Put the camera on a tripod in a dark room or outside at night. Try ISO 100–400, f/8, and Bulb or 10–30 s. Focus manually where the phone will be.
  • On the phone: brightness to full, Auto-Brightness, True Tone and Night Shift off, Portrait Orientation Lock on, Low Power Mode off.
  • Point the screen at the camera. Wear dark clothes and keep moving, and you will barely show up.
  • Run Sensors once to calibrate; it works out how you hold the phone. Use the live test to check the dot follows you.
  • Sweep across the full width in 1–3 s. Slower sweeps give brighter, smoother pictures. Going back and forth over the image builds up brightness.
  • If fast sweeps look dotted, that is the OLED's PWM flicker. Slow down, or turn it into a style with Dashes.
  • For a full-screen app: Safari Share → Add to Home Screen. The page must be served over https for the motion sensors.
